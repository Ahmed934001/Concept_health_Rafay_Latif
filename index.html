Our medical specialties

Comprehensive Care Designed Around Your Needs

Two specialties, one continuous relationship. Care that follows you through every stage of adulthood — and adapts as your needs change.

General Adult Internal Medicine

Comprehensive medical care focused on adult health, preventive care, the evaluation and management of medical conditions, and ongoing wellness. Our approach emphasizes understanding your medical history, identifying health concerns, and developing appropriate care plans based on your individual needs.

  • Preventive healthcare and recommended screenings
  • Chronic disease management — hypertension, diabetes, cholesterol
  • Comprehensive medical evaluation for overlapping symptoms
  • Medication review and ongoing adult healthcare
Explore Internal Medicine

Specialized Geriatric Medicine

As healthcare needs evolve with age, medical care often requires a more comprehensive perspective. Our geriatric focus addresses the complex interaction of medical conditions, medications, mobility, functional independence, and overall well-being.

  • Comprehensive geriatric assessment
  • Frailty prevention and falls risk evaluation
  • Complex chronic conditions and polypharmacy review
  • Transitions of care, goals of care & advance planning
Explore Geriatric Medicine

Preventive Healthcare

Health-risk assessments, recommended screenings, lifestyle factors and preventive planning — before problems announce themselves.

Chronic Disease Management

Regular monitoring, treatment adjustments and informed decision-making for long-term conditions.

Comprehensive Evaluation

When symptoms overlap or don't fit one diagnosis, we look at the whole picture — history, medications, exam and diagnostics.

Medication Review

How your medicines work together, whether doses still fit, and whether anything can safely be simplified.

Falls Risk Evaluation

Medical, functional and medication-related risk factors assessed together, with practical prevention strategies.

Frailty Prevention

Strength, function and nutrition reviewed together — because frailty sits on a spectrum that can be influenced.

Transitions of Care

Continuity between hospital, home and rehabilitation — with medication information that actually matches reality.

Goals of Care

Thoughtful conversations about what matters most, so future decisions reflect your values — not guesswork.

Your care journey

More Than Treating Conditions. Understanding People.

We believe effective healthcare begins with listening, continues through informed clinical decisions, and respects the individual needs of every patient. Here is exactly how that looks in practice.

01

We listen — properly

Your medical history and current concerns, in your words, without rushing.

Step 01 · Understanding

We listen — properly

Your medical history and current health concerns come first. Not a checklist — a conversation. This is where the useful details usually surface.

Step 02 · Prevention

We plan ahead, not just behind

Preventive healthcare and long-term wellness are discussed openly: which screenings are due, which lifestyle factors matter most for you, and what the next 12 months should look like.

Step 03 · Management

We support what's ongoing

Ongoing medical conditions get appropriate, consistent management — with treatment adjusted when the evidence, or your circumstances, change.

Step 04 · Function

We protect independence

As patients age, mobility and functional health matter as much as lab values. We consider both, because independence is a medical outcome too.

Step 05 · Partnership

We keep everyone informed

Informed decisions and clear communication — with you, and with family or caregivers involved when appropriate and with your consent.

Meet your physician

A Physician. An Educator. A Healthcare Leader.

Dr. Rafay Latif is a board-certified Internal Medicine and Geriatric Medicine physician with more than 15 years of experience in hospital-based medicine.

His professional background includes clinical practice in academic medical centers, Veterans Affairs healthcare settings, and community hospitals, as well as leadership roles in hospital medicine and medical education. With postgraduate training in Internal Medicine and Geriatric Medicine and a Master of Health Administration from Columbia University, Dr. Latif brings a comprehensive perspective to patient care.

Preparing for Your Appointment

  • A government-issued photo ID
  • Your insurance card, if applicable
  • A current list of medications and supplements
  • Relevant medical records or recent test results
  • Information about recent hospitalizations
  • A list of symptoms, concerns, or questions
  • Contact details of other clinicians involved in your care

For geriatric appointments, information about recent falls, mobility changes, daily functioning, or medication difficulties may also be helpful. Patients who would like a family member or caregiver to participate should contact the clinic to discuss appropriate arrangements.
